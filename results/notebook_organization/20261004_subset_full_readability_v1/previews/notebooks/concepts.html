Concepts used in the notebooks

Use this page when a term is unfamiliar. Each notebook explains the terms needed for its own task; you can read its explanation first and return here for context.

Data and evaluation

  • Metadata means recorded information such as age, sex and lesion location. An image model instead learns from pixels.
  • Target is the recorded label: 1 for malignant and 0 for benign. One row is usually one image; a patient may contribute several images.
  • Patient grouping keeps a person's images together when dividing data. Otherwise, a model could recognise information about a patient it has already seen, making evaluation look better than performance on new patients.
  • Fold means one group used for validation. Out-of-fold (OOF) predictions come from models fitted without the predicted patient's fold. Pooling all folds gives broader development evidence than inspecting just one fold.
  • Inner validation is a separate patient group inside the training portion. It helps compare settings or choose a saved model. Results on that same group remain development diagnostics because those decisions used its labels.
  • Reserved test is held back until the whole workflow is fixed. The planned final-evaluation notebook has no test results yet.

Reading model results

Term What to read Why it matters
Prediction score Higher means the model ranks an image as more likely malignant A score between 0 and 1 is not automatically a reliable risk probability
Average precision (AP) Higher means malignant images tend to appear earlier in the ranking Useful with rare malignant cases; compare with the malignant fraction of the evaluated sample
ROC-AUC Higher means stronger separation of malignant and benign scores; 0.5 is a chance-ranking reference It measures ranking, rather than the percentage classified correctly
Sensitivity / recall Fraction of malignant images flagged at a chosen cutoff Shows how many labelled malignant images the classification rule captures
Specificity Fraction of benign images left unflagged Shows the other side of the same cutoff tradeoff
Calibration Agreement between scores and observed malignant frequency On a reliability diagram, below the diagonal means scores exceed observed frequency
Brier score / log loss Lower means closer agreement between scores and labels These assess the score scale as well as prediction errors
Patient-bootstrap interval Range obtained by repeatedly resampling whole patients Keeps a patient's images together when describing uncertainty in a result; it is not an individual risk interval

Repeated image predictions

A checkpoint stores a trained model and its recorded settings. An epoch is one pass through the training data. Augmentation changes training images to vary their appearance; those changes must preserve the lesion and its label.

A logit is the model's raw output before a sigmoid converts it to a score between 0 and 1. BatchNorm standardises features using stored training statistics during evaluation. LayerNorm standardises a selected set of features within each example. These normalisation layers stay in evaluation mode during the dropout experiment so the intended change comes from dropout alone.

MC dropout repeatedly predicts the same image while randomly disabling some units in explicit dropout layers. TTA, or test-time augmentation, instead predicts a fixed set of permitted image views. Their standard deviations (SDs) measure different sources of prediction variation. A stable prediction can still be wrong, and a larger SD does not mean a larger melanoma risk.

Referral sets aside a fraction of images for hypothetical review. Coverage is the fraction retained by the model. Compare referral rankings at the same fraction using the same underlying scores. Lower error in the retained group does not measure what happens to referred images or prove that review helps.
