Image modelling for the Subset

Start with the question

The image models learn patterns in lesion pixels, rather than age, sex or body location. The aim is to rank malignant images higher while preserving patient separation. Start with Subset 09 for the basic EfficientNet-B0 pilot, then 10 for architecture comparisons. 11 checks whether stronger training transformations are visually safe; 12 records a deferred lighting experiment. 13 asks whether variation in an existing model's predictions helps prioritise images for review.

These notebooks read saved checkpoints, predictions, training histories and run manifests. A checkpoint stores learned model parameters; a manifest records which data and settings produced them. Reports save tables and figures. Notebook 13 runs inference, meaning prediction with an existing model, and saves a new directory for each run. See the notebook index for commands and status, and the concept guide for shared terms.

The present evidence is exploratory. Every current image pilot uses one inner-validation patient split. Those patients were excluded from fitting, but their labels chose pilot settings and epochs. That makes the scores useful for development diagnostics, without giving an unbiased final performance estimate. Outer fold 0 and the separately reserved test are both excluded. Full-dataset modelling, full outer OOF evaluation and fusion are not performed here. Historical d1/d2 artifact names remain unchanged even though notebook folders are now Subset and Full.

Read the figures

Figure What to compare Interpretation and limit
Learning curves Follow epoch (one training pass) horizontally and loss or AP vertically. Training and validation curves describe different patients. Falling training loss with worsening validation results suggests overfitting. Higher AP is better; lower loss is better. Losses with different positive weights are different objectives and should not be compared as if identical.
Precision–recall and ROC curves In precision–recall, compare precision at the same malignant recall. In ROC, compare malignant recall at the same benign false-positive rate. Curves nearer the top are preferable in PR, and top-left in ROC. AP is especially useful for rare malignant images. The PR prevalence line is the no-information reference for this split. These are ranking checks, not a chosen referral rule.
Confusion matrices Read actual and predicted labels from the axes; count malignant images predicted benign and benign images predicted malignant. The raw cutoff is fixed at 0.5 for an exploratory example. High accuracy can coexist with many missed malignant images.
Reliability plot Compare mean score horizontally with observed malignant fraction vertically and the diagonal. Check bin counts. Below the diagonal suggests overprediction; above suggests underprediction. Few malignant cases make bins noisy. These checks do not fit a calibrator or turn raw scores into validated probabilities.
Quality-review image grids Compare each transformed image with its original: lesion boundaries, pigment, texture and colours should remain plausible. A sharper or more varied image is not automatically safer. Clipping or reflected lesion fragments block the stronger condition before training.
Timing, memory and recipe comparisons Compare matched, completed recipes, with their actual epochs and run status. Faster or higher-scoring short pilots do not establish the final architecture. Deferred and incomplete conditions have no performance comparison.
Uncertainty distributions and score scatter Read standard deviation (SD) as spread across dropout passes or TTA views; compare spread with the underlying score. Larger spread means more sensitivity to the chosen perturbation. It is not a probability of error. MC dropout and TTA measure different kinds of variation.
Pass-count convergence Compare means, SDs and rankings at 5, 10 and 20 passes with the 30-pass reference. Smaller differences suggest stability within this run; 30 passes are not a proof of full convergence.
Referral / retained-error curves Move along the shared fraction set aside for review. Compare retained error and malignant images referred at the same fraction. Lower retained error is useful only alongside who was removed; referring malignant images changes the retained case mix. Compare MC/TTA with ordinary score, entropy and random ranking. Referral is a simulation, without measured reviewer benefit.
Paired-bootstrap differences Read the named method minus comparator and its patient-bootstrap interval. Lower retained error differences favour the first method; higher malignant-referral differences favour it. An interval crossing zero does not clearly resolve the difference. Predictions are held fixed, so training and pilot-selection variation are not covered.

Preprocessing prepares an image deterministically; augmentation varies fitting images to help generalisation. MC dropout switches on only trained dropout layers during repeated prediction. Test-time augmentation (TTA) predicts separate flipped views with dropout off. The details below explain the recorded experiments and reproducibility rules; later stages are explicitly marked as plans.

Training and checkpoint rules

Compare backbone learning rates 3e-5 and 1e-4, each with ordinary BCE and moderate positive weighting sqrt(negative_count / positive_count). Compute weights from fitting images only. Each recipe gets six epochs, including one classifier-only warmup epoch. The classifier learning rate is 1e-3; AdamW weight decay is 1e-4. BatchNorm statistics and affine parameters stay frozen.

Use automatic mixed precision on CUDA, clipping gradients at 1.0. Begin with microbatch 64 and effective batch 64. If a CUDA memory error occurs, roll back the epoch, halve the microbatch down to four and use gradient accumulation to preserve the effective batch. Account for the actual sample count in the last accumulation window.

The schedule is declared before training: one warmup epoch, then 29 cosine fine-tuning epochs, ending at 10% of the original learning rates. A six-epoch pilot does not compress this into a six-epoch decay. A compatible continuation therefore follows the same learning-rate trajectory.

Save both the highest-AP checkpoint and the latest epoch-boundary checkpoint. The latter contains optimiser, scheduler, scaler and random-generator states. An exact AP tie keeps the earlier checkpoint. A changed input, recipe, code or execution environment requires a separate run or an explicit incompatibility error. Prediction-only execution must preserve training history.

The 60-minute compute allocation stops between epochs. If any of the four trials is unfinished, the comparison remains incomplete and has no declared winner. Completed trials are not compared with shorter trials as equal runs.

The CLI deliberately enforces this six-epoch pilot. A longer experiment must be introduced as a separate stage with its own budget and output identity. The shared recipe trainer supports the fixed 30-epoch horizon, but extending a run must not overwrite the six-epoch pilot's interpretation or comparison summary.

What the pilot can establish

The saved results show training speed, memory use, learning curves and whether the pipeline runs correctly. They provide preliminary evidence about B0 settings. They cannot establish the optimal architecture or an unbiased performance estimate after selecting epochs on those same validation cases.

AP measures how well melanoma images are ranked when they are rare. ROC-AUC provides another ranking view. Log loss, Brier score and reliability bins describe the raw scores' probability quality. Include the validation prevalence and a constant fitting-prevalence baseline.

Reliability bins are calibration checks, not a fitted calibration model. Do not fit a calibrator or select a threshold on the pilot validation labels: those labels already choose epochs and settings. In the later nested workflow, three fixed-duration inner refits provide training-only OOF predictions for sigmoid recalibration (C=1) and an academic 80%-recall threshold scenario. Failed or non-positive-slope recalibration retains raw results and records the failure; no calibrated result is silently fabricated.

If the provisional winner's best epoch is five or six, recommend extending compatible training to a 30-epoch cap. An earlier peak suggests that more epochs may offer little improvement on this split. Either observation remains exploratory; repeat validation on the other inner folds in the later stage.

Preprocessing

Hair removal is optional: at 224 pixels use grayscale black-hat detection with a 17×17 elliptical kernel, threshold above 20, one 3×3 dilation and Telea inpainting radius three. Empty masks leave the image unchanged. Masks exceeding 10% of pixels return the original image with an audit flag. Inspect originals, masks, reconstructions and all flagged fitting examples for lost lesion detail.
